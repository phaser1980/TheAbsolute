What it is

An Army assessment

Written for US Army Intelligence and Security Command on 9 June 1983; later released through the CIA Reading Room.

What it is not

Proof of a simulation

The report blends real concepts, period neuroscience, metaphysics and speculative leaps. Declassification does not validate every claim.

Powerful ideas.
Firm ground.

The experience works best when wonder and critical thinking remain online together. These principles shape the entire product.

Claims stay attributed

“The report proposes” is different from “science proves.” The interface preserves that distinction everywhere.

Practice before mythology

Relaxation, focused attention, imagery and journalling are useful without requiring belief in a holographic universe.

Agency without blame

Visualisation may guide behaviour and perception. It does not mean people caused illness, trauma or misfortune through imperfect thoughts.

No medical promises

Historical claims about healing are presented as claims. The site does not diagnose, treat or replace qualified care.

Return is part of the method

Every session ends by orienting to body, room, time and ordinary responsibilities. Expansion without reintegration is incomplete.

Record what happened

Users can later compare expectations, impressions and outcomes—reducing hindsight bias and making the exploration more honest.

The Absolute is an independent educational interpretation of a historical document. It is not affiliated with the CIA, US Army, Monroe Institute or Hemi-Sync. It does not establish that reality is a simulation, that consciousness controls external events, or that paranormal abilities are reproducible.
